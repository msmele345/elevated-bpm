Lesson 01 · 17 minutes · First session

Build the pulse.

Turn sixteen anonymous buttons into one readable bar, then place the four kicks that make techno feel grounded from the first click.

  • 16-step grid
  • four on the floor
  • tempo
  • ear training

The one idea

Four beats. Four landmarks.

One bar contains sixteen equal steps. Each beat owns four steps, so the beat starts land on 1, 5, 9, and 13. Put a kick on every beat start and you have four on the floor.

Guided lab

From silence to floor

  1. 00:00–02:00Open the Techno track. Set Master · Tempo near 132 BPM, press Play, and tap your foot four times: “1, 2, 3, 4.”
  2. 02:00–05:00Point to the four color groups. Say their first steps aloud: 1, 5, 9, 13. Those are the downbeats.
  3. 05:00–10:00In Drum Machine · DR-909, clear any existing Kick hits, then turn on exactly steps 1, 5, 9, 13. Count four complete loops.
  4. 10:00–14:00Clear step 9 for one loop and notice the missing floor. Restore it. Move 13 to 14 for one loop, then restore 13. Make one change at a time.
  5. 14:00–17:00Move Tempo from 120 to 140 BPM while playing. Name what changed—speed—and what stayed fixed—the hit locations.
Listen for: a clear physical pulse that survives everything happening around it. When one downbeat disappears, your body should notice before your eyes do.

Ear experiment

Break it to understand it.

Target state

1 · 5 · 9 · 13

One unaccented kick at the start of each beat. All other Kick steps dark.

Vocabulary

Three words

Beat

The regular pulse you count.

Tempo

Pulse speed in beats per minute.

Step

One sixteenth of this one-bar grid.

Retrieval check

No peeking.

Which step begins beat 3? What changes when BPM changes? What does not?

Reveal answer

Beat 3 begins at step 9. BPM changes playback speed; the programmed step positions stay the same.
