Lesson 03 · 18 minutes · Beginner

Write bass in the gaps.

Give the kick the downbeats and let the bass answer between them. Then turn one repeated pitch into a line with direction.

  • negative space
  • pitch
  • note length
  • one-variable tests

The one idea

Kick and bass take turns.

Start the bass halfway between the four kicks. The two low-frequency parts interlock instead of arriving on every downbeat together.

Guided lab

Place rhythm. Then shape pitch.

  1. 00:00–03:00Run the Lesson 2 drums. Find the empty midpoint between every two kicks: 3, 7, 11, 15.
  2. 03:00–08:00In Bass Line · BL-303, turn on those four steps. Keep their default C pitch for one loop and hear the kick–bass handoff.
  3. 08:00–13:00Leave step 3 at C. Raise step 7 by 3 semitones to E♭, step 11 by 7 to G, and step 15 by 10 to B♭. Use Arrow Up while each placed note retains focus.
  4. 13:00–16:00Use Arrow Right on step 15 to make it two steps long. Compare it with the one-step version and keep the version that makes the next kick strongest.
  5. 16:00–18:00Move only step 7 up one semitone, listen for two loops, then move it back. Name which version adds more tension.
Listen for: the line’s contour—anchor, rise, rise, tension—plus clean space around each kick.

Starting rule

Interlock first.

Placing bass between kicks is not a law. It is a clear starting point that lets a beginner hear the relationship before breaking it.

Finish line

Can you hum it?

Retrieval check

Name the data.

What does a bass step store that a drum step does not?

Reveal answer

A bass step stores pitch and note length. A drum step stores on/off and accent.
