Lesson 04 · 17 minutes · Beginner

Shape an acid voice.

Stop turning every knob at once. Give each control one job, compare two deliberate patches, then perform a movement you can explain.

  • cutoff
  • resonance
  • decay
  • prediction

The control surface

Brightness. Edge. Time.

Cutoffdark ↔ bright
Resonanceround ↔ squelchy
Decayplucky ↔ long

Interaction: resonance emphasizes the boundary chosen by cutoff. Decay changes how long each event occupies the spaces in your rhythm.

Guided lab

Two patches, one moving control.

  1. 00:00–04:00Run your Lesson 3 bassline. Sweep one knob across a wide range while the other two stay still. Describe the change before moving on.
  2. 04:00–08:00Build Patch A · dark and tight: Cutoff 300–600 Hz, Resonance 3–7 Q, Decay 0.10–0.22 s. Record your exact values.
  3. 08:00–12:00Build Patch B · bright acid: Cutoff 900–2,500 Hz, Resonance 11–16 Q, Decay 0.18–0.38 s. Move one control per loop.
  4. 12:00–15:00Return to a dark cutoff. Over four loops: hold, open gradually, keep opening, then close back. Leave Resonance and Decay still.
  5. 15:00–17:00Predict each move aloud—brighter, more ring, or shorter—then test it on the corresponding knob.
Listen for: whether the result changed in the direction you predicted. Surprise is useful evidence, not failure.

Diagnostic order

Change one thing.

If a patch is too piercing, lower Resonance first. If pitch is unclear, raise Cutoff slightly. If notes smear, shorten Decay.

Finish line

Predict, then touch.

Retrieval check

Choose the knob.

More brightness? Harder ring at the filter edge? More silence between notes?

Reveal answer

Raise Cutoff. Raise Resonance. Shorten Decay.
