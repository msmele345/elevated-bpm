Finish. Then perform.

The capstone pattern

Say less, land harder.

A stab is a short, emphatic chord-like hit. Place only two: late in each half of the bar, where they can answer rather than cover the rhythm.

Guided lab

Program, play, isolate, perform.

  1. 00:00–05:00In Chord Stab · CS-08, turn on steps 7 and 15. Listen twice before transposing either with Arrow Up/Down.
  2. 05:00–08:00Hold computer keys A + D + G together for a live major chord. Play it once on beat 1, wait a bar, then once on beat 4.
  3. 08:00–12:00Solo Kick, then add Closed Hat to the solo set. Clear all solos. Mute Clap and Open Hat one at a time, then restore both.
  4. 12:00–17:00Perform the four-stage energy arc below. Count four loops per stage and make only the named changes.
  5. 17:00–20:00Listen untouched for two loops. Explain each layer aloud. Create a Share URL if you want a portable snapshot.
Listen for: intention. Every added, muted, filtered, or driven layer should change the groove in a way you can name.

Four-stage performance

One bar can still have an arc.

  1. 01 · EstablishMaster Filter open, Drive near 0%. Play four loops unchanged.
  2. 02 · ReduceMute Closed Hat and Open Hat. Let the low and mid layers feel heavier.
  3. 03 · BuildRestore the hats. Slowly close, then reopen, Master Filter.
  4. 04 · ReleaseAdd modest Drive and play A + D + G on the first beat. Return Drive near 0% afterward.

Live keyboard

A · D · G

Hold all three together, then release together. Sequenced stabs repeat; the live chord happens only when your hands choose it.

Safety check

Return neutral.

Retrieval check

Explain the tools.

Why solo temporarily? Which master control darkens the entire mix? Why only two stabs?

Reveal answer

Solo exposes timing and tone. Master Filter removes high-frequency content. Sparse stabs preserve space and make each hit matter.
