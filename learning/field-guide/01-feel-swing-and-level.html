Field guide 01 · Any level

Shuffle it. Balance it.

Swing moves the offbeat 16ths. Level sets how loud each voice is. Neither touches your programming, so change both while the loop plays and undo either by ear.

  • level

The one idea

Swing is when. Level is how loud.

Swing holds back every offbeat 16th — steps 2, 4, 6 … 16 — while the steps on the beat stay locked. Every sequenced voice swings together; what you play live never does.

Control behavior

Swing, in the transport strip.

A small knob labelled Swing, right of the BPM readout. 50% (straight) to 75% (heavy) — the numbers an MPC or a TR-8 shows.

SettingWhat you hear
50%Dead straight. Every 16th on the grid. Where every project starts.
54–58%A slight push. Looser, but still reads as straight.
66%Triplet feel. The offbeat sits two-thirds through each 8th.
75%Dotted feel. A heavy limp.

Try it

  1. Program a closed hat on every step, 1–16, and press Play.
  2. Turn Swing slowly from 50% to 75%. Listen to the even ticks turn into a skip.
  3. Settle between 54% and 62% and play for four loops.
  4. Return to 50% and listen to how stiff it sounds now.
Playhead: the light keeps sweeping the straight grid. At heavy swing an offbeat sounds up to half a 16th after the light passes it. That is expected.

Mixer drill

A Level knob on every voice.

Eleven voices, one set of controls: the knob always sits right after that voice’s M and S — at the end of each drum and pad lane’s label strip, and in the Bass Line and Chord Stab panel headers.

The readout is in dB: 0.0 dB is unity and the default, the top is +6.0 dB, and all the way down reads −∞ dB. Most of the travel sits between −24 dB and +6 dB, where mixing happens.

  1. Press Play on a groove with kick and hats.
  2. Bring the Closed Hat’s Level down to about −6 dB. The kick comes forward without getting louder.
  3. Bring the Bass Line’s Level up to +2 dB and back to 0.0 dB. Decide which sits better under the kick.
  4. Pull a lane to −∞ dB, then compare it with pressing M. Both are silent, but M leaves the Level where you set it, so pressing it again brings the lane back exactly as loud as before.
Listen for: accents. An accented step stays louder than a plain one at any Level, so turning the hats down keeps their dynamics.

Mixer drill

Mute and solo on the bass and the stab.

Bass Line and Chord Stab now carry M and S beside their Level knobs. One rule covers all eleven voices:

A silenced panel or lane dims, so you can see which voices are out of the mix.

Keyboard

Every knob, same keys.

↑/→ and ↓/← nudge 2% of the travel, Page Up/Page Down jump 10%, Home and End park at the ends — 50%/75% on Swing, −∞/+6 dB on Level. Hold Shift while dragging for fine control.

What travels

Saved and shared.

Swing, every Level, every mute and solo are saved with your project and carried in share links and bundles. The person you send a beat to hears your shuffle and your mix.

Retrieval check

No peeking.

At 66% swing, does step 5’s kick move? You turned the Open Hat to −12 dB — are its accents still louder? Kick is soloed; Bass Line is muted and soloed. What plays?

Reveal answer

No — step 5 is on the beat. Yes — Level sits after the accent. Kick and bass — solo overrides mute, and more than one solo is allowed.
