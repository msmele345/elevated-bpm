# Field Guide 01 — Feel: Swing and Level

**Covers:** the **Swing** knob in the transport strip, a **Level** knob on every voice, **M** and **S** on Bass Line and Chord Stab  
**Level:** Any  
**Primary visual aid:** [Open the HTML page](01-feel-swing-and-level.html)

## The one idea

Swing changes *when* the offbeat 16ths land. Level changes *how loud* each voice is. Neither touches your programming, so you can change both while the loop plays and undo either by ear.

## Swing

**Where:** the transport strip, right of the BPM readout. It is a small knob labelled **Swing** with a percentage readout.

**Range:** 50% (straight) to 75% (heavy). The numbers mean what they mean on an MPC or a TR-8.

| Setting | What you hear |
|---|---|
| **50%** | Dead straight. Every 16th on the grid. This is where every project starts. |
| **54–58%** | A slight push. The groove loosens but still sounds straight. |
| **66%** | Triplet feel. The offbeat 16th sits two-thirds of the way through each 8th. |
| **75%** | Dotted feel. A heavy limp. |

What moves and what does not:

- Only the **offbeat 16ths** move — steps 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14 and 16. Steps 1, 3, 5 … 15 stay on the grid, so the kick never drifts.
- **Every sequenced voice** swings together: drums, sampler pads, bass and stab. The bass stays locked to the kick.
- **What you play live** does not swing. Keys and pads sound the moment you press them.
- The **playhead** keeps sweeping the straight grid. At heavy swing the sound of an offbeat lands up to half a 16th after the light passes it. That is expected.

### Try it

1. Program a closed hat on every step (1–16) and press **Play**.
2. Turn **Swing** slowly from 50% to 75%. Listen to the even ticks turn into a skip.
3. Settle somewhere between 54% and 62% and play for four loops.
4. Return to 50% and listen to how stiff it sounds now.

**Keyboard:** Tab to the Swing knob. **↑/→** and **↓/←** nudge it by 2% of its travel, **Page Up/Down** by 10%, **Home** sets it to 50% and **End** to 75%. Hold **Shift** while dragging for fine control.

Swing is saved with your project and travels in share links and bundles, so the person you send a beat to hears the same shuffle.

## Level

**Where:** every voice on the Deck has one, and it always sits right after that voice's **M** and **S**:

- **Drum lanes** (Kick, Clap, Closed Hat, Open Hat, Perc) — at the end of each lane's label strip.
- **Sampler pad lanes** (Pad 1–4) — the same place, once the pad has a lane.
- **Bass Line** and **Chord Stab** — in the panel header.

That is eleven voices, mixed with one set of controls.

**Readout:** in dB. **0.0 dB** is unity and the default. The top is **+6.0 dB**. Turned all the way down it reads **−∞ dB** — silent. Most of the knob's travel is spent between −24 dB and +6 dB, where mixing happens; the last stretch below −24 dB just fades to silence.

Things worth knowing:

- **Accents survive.** An accented step stays louder than an unaccented one at any Level. Turning the hats down keeps their dynamics.
- **Live playing goes through it too.** A stab chord you play on the keyboard is as loud as Chord Stab's Level says.
- **Moving it never clicks.** Level ramps smoothly while the loop runs.
- The **Master** strip's Filter and Drive still shape the whole mix after every Level.

### Try it

1. Press **Play** on a groove with kick and hats.
2. Bring the Closed Hat's Level down to about **−6 dB**. The kick comes forward without getting louder.
3. Bring the Bass Line's Level up to **+2 dB** and back to **0.0 dB**. Decide which sits better under the kick.
4. Pull a lane all the way to **−∞ dB** and compare it with pressing **M**. Both are silent, but **M** leaves the Level where you set it, so pressing it again brings the lane back exactly as loud as before.

**Keyboard:** the same keys as Swing. **Home** goes to −∞ dB, **End** to +6 dB.

## Mute and solo on the bass and the stab

Bass Line and Chord Stab now have **M** and **S** in their panel headers, beside their Level knobs. The rule is the same across all eleven voices:

- **M** silences that voice.
- **S** solos it. If any voice is soloed, only soloed voices play.
- You can solo more than one voice at once — solo Kick and Bass Line to hear just the low end.
- **Solo beats mute.** A voice that is both muted and soloed plays.
- **M** and **S** apply to live playing as well. A muted Chord Stab stays silent on the keyboard.

A silenced panel or lane dims, so you can see at a glance which voices are out of the mix.

## Retrieval check

Answer before you open the answers:

1. You set Swing to 66%. Does step 5's kick move?
2. You turned the Open Hat down to −12 dB. Are its accents still louder than its plain steps?
3. Kick is soloed and Bass Line is muted *and* soloed. What do you hear?

1. No. Step 5 is on the beat. Only the even-numbered steps move.
2. Yes. Level sits after the accent, so the gap between them is kept.
3. Kick and bass. Solo overrides mute, and more than one solo is allowed.
