Field guide 03 · Any level

Four slots, one machine.

Slots A–D hold four patterns. Switch between them on the bar line, copy one to make a variation, and send the whole bank to a friend.

  • slots
  • queued switch

The one idea

A slot holds what is played. The Deck holds how it sounds.

Each slot holds every drum lane with its accents, every pad lane, the bass line with its pitches, lengths, accents and slides, and the stab lane. Tempo, swing, every Level, mute and solo, the bass patch, the Master macros, the FX and the pad sounds stay put when you switch, so a variation always sounds like the same machine.

A new project starts with the demo groove in A and B–D empty. A beat made before v3 is now slot A, unchanged. The Deck remembers your slot when you reload.

Control behavior

Reading the slot buttons.

What you seeWhat it means
Pressed-in buttonThe selected slot — the grid shows it and your edits go to it.
Steady LEDThe playing slot — the one you hear.
Blinking LEDA queued slot — it takes over at the end of the bar.
Dim letterAn empty slot — no step is on in any lane.

A screen reader hears all of it in the button’s name, for example “Slot B, selected, queued, empty”.

Guided lab

Switch on the bar line.

While stopped, a slot switches at once. While playing, it is queued: the current slot plays to the end of the bar and the new one takes over on step 1. The grid shows the queued slot straight away, so you can get it ready, and a notice hangs under the strip:

Editing B · hearing A until the bar ends
  1. Press Play with the demo in A.
  2. Press B. The grid goes empty; the demo keeps playing. Watch B blink.
  3. At the end of the bar, B comes in. The loop is silent.
  4. Press A to bring the demo back at the next bar line.

Changed your mind? Press a different slot to queue that one instead, the playing slot to cancel, or Stop to switch at once.

Guided lab

Copy and Clear.

Copy — a variation in two clicks.

  1. Select the slot to copy from.
  2. Press Copy. The other slots blink to say Copy is armed.
  3. Press the slot to copy onto. It gets an exact copy and becomes the selected slot — while playing, that means it is queued.

If the target already has programming, the strip asks Overwrite B? with Overwrite and Cancel. Focus lands on Cancel, so a stray Enter never wipes a pattern. Press Copy again or Escape to back out; pressing the slot you are copying from does nothing.

Clear — start a slot over. Select the slot, press Clear, answer Clear A? with Clear or Cancel. Only its steps go; the slot and the other three stay. Clear does nothing on an empty slot.

Patch notebook

A breakdown and a drop.

SlotJobHow to build it
AMain grooveYour full beat
BBreakdownCopy A onto B, then take out the kick
CBuildCopy B onto C, then add an open hat and a few bass notes
DDropCopy A onto D, then add one more percussion hit and an accent or two
Perform it: A for four bars, then B, C, D, back to A. Press each slot a little before the bar line you want it on — the bank does not chain patterns for you.

Lessons

Built in B still counts.

Lessons check the selected slot. When you open someone else’s beat, any lesson their bank already satisfies — in any slot — is held back, so switching to their B never earns you a lesson you did not do.

What travels

Links and bundles.

Retrieval check

Which slot?

Playing A, you press C — which slot do your clicks change? You copy A onto B and turn the kick’s Level down — is A’s kick quieter too? Your link holds pattern A only — how do you send all four?

Reveal answer

C — edits follow the selected slot at once; you hear them when C comes in. Yes — Level belongs to the Deck, not a slot. Export a bundle from Share ▾.
